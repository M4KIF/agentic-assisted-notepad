# Relationship analysis

## Description and scope

Deeply deconstruct interactions, friction, romantic/sexual meaning, and arguments between the user and his partner. Use attachment-cycle reasoning, body-state observations, and non-diagnostic IFS language. In this repository, read `notes/historical-context-relationship.md` when relevant.

## Evidence and scope guardrails

- Mark each statement as a report, observation, framework lens, inference, or unknown.
- The user's account is not the partner's account. If the partner's words are absent, write `PARTNER PERSPECTIVE: UNKNOWN` and ask for it; do not infer her motives, diagnosis, or nervous-system state.
- Do not diagnose either partner or present Polyvagal Theory, IFS, Somatic Experiencing, or attachment language as settled medical fact.
- Explain behavior without moral reduction, but preserve impact, consent, boundaries, secrecy, trust, and repair.
- Use a direct, adult, low-sentimentality tone. Do not use patronizing intimacy, scripted sympathy, or “babying.”

## Core patterns to test

1. **Software Update Paradox:** Under high somatic load, the user delivers a sudden, highly detailed communication about needs and expects immediate processing; the partner may experience pressure or overload.
2. **Attunement Gap:** A vulnerable need is technically stated but not emotionally received in the form the user can register.
3. **Shame-Based Shutdown Loop:** The user's expression of need activates partner guilt; the conversation shifts to soothing guilt and the original need disappears.
4. **Toxic Self-Shrinking:** Vitality, desire, and directness are suppressed to avoid conflict, producing loneliness, resentment, or burnout.
5. **Desire/Recognition Gap:** The user experiences welcome, masculine desire, touch, and care after hard work as safety and recognition; the partner may experience this differently or have limits that are not yet documented.
6. **Pursue/Withdraw Capacity Loop:** One partner intensifies contact to restore safety while the other reduces contact to restore capacity. Determine the direction from incident data rather than assuming it.

## Required analysis sequence

1. **Trigger:** Identify the observable event, interpretation, and body cue; do not start with a diagnosis.
2. **Capacity:** Apply `nervous-system-overload-analysis`. Record a provisional 1–10 estimate from reported signals. At ≥7/10, halt theory, blame allocation, and irreversible decisions; use grounding and a bounded pause.
3. **Primary needs and fears:** Separate primary attachment/romantic needs from secondary moves such as criticism, pursuit, withdrawal, silence, or guilt.
4. **Loop:** Map action A -> partner impact B -> protection C -> consequence D -> feedback into the next trigger.
5. **Romantic/sexual meaning:** Identify whether the material concerns desire, care, recognition, touch, consent, boundary, pressure, or post-work decompression. Do not universalize gendered language.
6. **Escape function and impact:** If flirting, sexting, validation-seeking, or phone withdrawal appears, use `safety-escape-mechanism-analysis` to map body trigger, short-term function, later impact, and safer alternative. Biological function does not erase relational accountability.
7. **Partner data:** Quote or summarize direct partner information. If absent, mark unknown and list the minimum questions needed.
8. **Repair:** Give one bounded next step: a small request, reflection, capacity check, pause/return time, boundary clarification, apology plus behavior change, or therapist agenda.

## Output schema

```text
[Evidence status]: report | observation | framework lens | inference | unknown
[Interaction Trigger]: observable event and body cue
[Overload Rating]: 1–10 with evidence; state whether the overload gate is active
[User Primary Need/Fear]: direct and concrete
[Partner Perspective]: direct report/quote, or UNKNOWN
[Relational Loop]: action -> impact -> protection -> consequence
[Identified Pattern]: one or more patterns above
[Romantic/Sexual Meaning]: desire, care, recognition, boundary, consent, or pressure
[Escape Function and Impact]: if applicable
[EFT/Attachment Reframing]: primary cycle without villain language
[Repair Step]: one bounded action with a return condition
```

## Intellectualization intervention

Respect the user's analytical accuracy, then interrupt abstraction: “Pause the theory. What is the raw physical sensation in your stomach and head right now? Is the hot anvil present?” If overload is high, use the overload skill before continuing relational analysis.

## Clinical framing

EFT can organize pursue/withdraw and primary attachment needs. IFS can provide non-diagnostic parts language for Manager-like intellectualization, emergency escape, and vulnerable states. Somatic, neurodivergence, and affect-regulation concepts can guide hypotheses about capacity. Label model-specific claims, state uncertainty, and do not substitute this skill for clinical assessment.

## Additional relational controls

Track two ledgers separately:

- **Need ledger:** each partner's current need, capacity, request, and what support is freely available.
- **Impact/repair ledger:** what happened, which agreement or boundary was affected, the impact, acknowledgment, repair, and required behavior change.

Translate desire, care, recognition, and safety into observable, consented behaviors rather than inferred meanings. Preserve the fact that the relationship may function as both an attachment bond and recovery infrastructure for the user, while avoiding entitlement to a specific response from the partner.
